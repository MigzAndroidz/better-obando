# Join Clean-Up Drives and Mangrove Reforestation in Obando

> Volunteer community river cleanups, coastal estuarine rehabilitation, and mangrove planting along the Obando and Binuangan river corridors.

---

## Overview

The estuarine and coastal ecosystems of the **Municipality of Obando** represent the town's natural shield against storm surges and tidal waves. Mangrove forests (bakawan) along the coastal shores of **Brgy. Binuangan** and **Brgy. Salambao** provide critical nursery habitats for bangus fry, crabs, and marine fish, while stabilizing coastal dikes against erosion.

The **Municipal Environment and Natural Resources Office (MENRO)** of Obando, in partnership with the **Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR Region III)**, civil society groups, schools, and youth organizations, regularly organizes volunteer cleanup drives and mangrove planting campaigns.

---

## Key Environmental Initiatives in Obando

### 1. Obando River & Estero Rehabilitation Cleanups

- **Weekly "Tapat Ko, Linis Ko" Barangay Drives:** Held every Saturday morning across all 11 barangays to clear floating plastic waste, water hyacinths (water lilies), and solid refuse from drainage canals before they enter the Obando River.
- **International Coastal Cleanup (ICC - September):** Large-scale mobilization of students, municipal employees, civic groups, and fisherfolk associations clearing plastic debris from riverbanks and the Manila Bay foreshore.
- **Water Hyacinth Harvester Operations:** Municipal mechanical harvesters clearing clogged water hyacinth mats that choke river channels and obstruct fishing motorboats.

### 2. Mangrove Reforestation (Bakawan Planting)

- Planting native mangrove species (_Rhizophora mucronata_ / Bakawan babae, _Rhizophora apiculata_ / Bakawan lalake, and _Avicennia marina_ / Api-api) along river easements and coastal mudflats in Binuangan and Salambao.
- Cultivating coastal greenbelt buffer zones to mitigate storm surge velocity and filter silt before it reaches Manila Bay coral and seagrass areas.

### 3. Urban Tree Planting & Green Corridors

- Planting native windbreak trees and flowering species (such as Banaba, Narra, and Talisay) along elevated dikes, public school perimeters, and municipal park grounds.

## How to Volunteer and Participate

### For Individuals, Students, and Families:

- **Join Scheduled Barangay Cleanups:** Sign up at your local Barangay Hall or join the environmental youth corps during designated community service weekends.
- **School YES-O & Scouting:** Public and private school students earn civic community hours by participating in LGU-endorsed mangrove planting missions.

### For Civic Organizations, NGOs, and Corporate CSR Partners:

- Corporate firms, church associations, and socio-civic groups seeking to conduct Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) tree planting or river cleanups must coordinate with Obando MENRO at least **two (2) weeks in advance**.
- MENRO provides:
  - Mangrove propagules / saplings sourced from accredited coastal nurseries.
  - Designated planting mudflat zones with boat logistics to Binuangan.
  - Technical briefing on proper mangrove planting depth, spacing, and post-planting monitoring.
  - Certificates of Environmental Stewardship for participating groups.

## What to Wear and Bring on Planting Day

- **Footwear:** Sturdy aqua shoes, old sneakers, or neoprene booties (mudflats contain sharp shells; avoid barefoot walking or loose slippers that get stuck in deep mud).
- **Attire:** Long-sleeved rashguard or light breathable cotton shirts, long pants/leggings to protect against sun and insect bites.
- **Protection:** Wide-brimmed hat, reef-safe sunscreen, insect repellent.
- **Hydration:** Reusable water tumbler (single-use plastic bottled water is discouraged during cleanups).
- **Gloves:** Heavy-duty rubberized or cotton gardening gloves for handling plastic debris and propagules.
