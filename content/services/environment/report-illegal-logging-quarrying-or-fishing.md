# Report Illegal Fishing, Mangrove Destruction, and River Encroachment in Obando

> Enforcement procedures for reporting electro-fishing, fine-mesh nets, unauthorized mangrove cutting, and river obstruction to Bantay Dagat and MENRO.

---

## Overview

The fisheries and aquatic resources of Obando are protected under **Republic Act 8550 as amended by Republic Act 10654 (The Philippine Fisheries Code)** and the **Obando Municipal Coastal and Fisheries Code**.

Destructive fishing methods and the unauthorized destruction of mangrove forests directly endanger the livelihoods of local artisanal fisherfolk and compromise Obando's natural defense against storm surges.

The **Obando Bantay Dagat Task Force**, in close coordination with the **Philippine National Police Maritime Group (PNP-MG)**, the **Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR)**, and the **Municipal Environment and Natural Resources Office (MENRO)**, actively patrols Obando's municipal waters and river channels to enforce environmental laws.

---

## Prohibited Ecological Activities in Obando

| Violation                                       | Description                                                                                                                    | Legal Consequence                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Electro-Fishing ("Kuryente")**                | Using electric currents from car batteries, inverters, or generators to stun or kill fish in rivers and fishpond gates         | Heavy fines, confiscation of fishing vessel and gear, and imprisonment |
| **Use of Fine Mesh Nets**                       | Using nets with mesh sizes smaller than three (3) centimeters, which trap juvenile fish fry and fingerlings                    | Confiscation of nets and administrative fines                          |
| **Use of Noxious or Poisonous Substances**      | Using chemicals, sodium cyanide, or plant poisons (such as tubli) to harvest fish                                              | Criminal prosecution under RA 10654                                    |
| **Cutting or Destruction of Mangroves**         | Felling, uprooting, or clearing mangrove trees for charcoal, firewood, or unauthorized fishpond expansion                      | Imprisonment under Presidential Decree 705 and Republic Act 7161       |
| **Illegal River Encroachment & Dike Expansion** | Constructing unauthorized bamboo stakes, fish pens, dikes, or structures that obstruct natural river navigation and tidal flow | Demolition of structures at owner's expense and municipal citation     |

## Step-by-Step Reporting Protocol

### Step 1: Collect Factual Information Safely

Do not confront violators directly, as illegal fishing operations may involve dangerous electrical equipment or hostile actors. Note down:

- **Location:** River tributary name, nearest bridge, landmark, or coastal coordinates near Binuangan/Salambao.
- **Boat Information:** Color, engine type, outrigger details, boat registration numbers, or vessel name.
- **Date and Time of Activity:** Illegal fishing frequently occurs under cover of darkness (late night or pre-dawn tides).
- **Photographic or Video Evidence:** If safe to obtain from a distance.

### Step 2: Immediate Report Dispatch

- **Emergency Waterborne Operations (Bantay Dagat & Police):**
  - Call the **Obando Municipal Police Station:** **(02) 8292-2043**
  - Alert the **Obando MDRRMO Emergency Operations Center:** **(02) 8281-5816**
- **Administrative & Environmental Complaints (MENRO):**
  - For mangrove cutting, illegal dikes, or chemical dumping, file a formal complaint with **MENRO** at the Obando Municipal Hall.

---

## What Happens to Confiscated Fish and Equipment?

- **Apprehended Vessels and Gear:** Confiscated fishing gear, electrical inverters, batteries, and boats are impounded in the municipal police custody yard as evidence for formal inquest.
- **Seized Fish Catches:** Fish seized from illegal operations are inspected by the Municipal Health Officer. If certified safe for human consumption, they are immediately donated to municipal social welfare institutions, daycare feeding programs, or charitable centers, with formal inventory receipts signed.
- **Fisheries Adjudication Board (FAB):** Administrative cases are heard before the Municipal Fisheries Adjudication Board to assess statutory fines and license cancellations.
