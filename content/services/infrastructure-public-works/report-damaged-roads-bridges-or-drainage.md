# Report Damaged Roads, Bridges, and Drainage in Obando

> How to report road potholes, broken river dikes, clogged culverts, and malfunctioning streetlights to the Obando Municipal Engineering Office.

---

## Overview

The low-lying coastal terrain of the **Municipality of Obando** subjects local roads, drainage culverts, tidal floodgates, and river dikes to continuous exposure to saline floodwaters and heavy vehicular traffic.

The **Municipal Engineering Office (MEO)** of Obando is responsible for the maintenance, repair, and engineering inspection of municipal public works, bridges, drainage systems, and public street lighting across the 11 barangays. Major national highways (such as the main J.P. Rizal arterial corridor) are maintained in close coordination with the **Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH Bulacan 1st District Engineering Office)**.

Citizen reports enable maintenance teams to rapidly dispatch repair crews before road depressions or dike cracks widen into dangerous hazards.

---

## Common Infrastructure Issues to Report

- **Road Potholes & Pavement Cracking:** Dangerous depressions on municipal roads, alley pavements, and bridge approaches.
- **Clogged or Collapsed Drainage Culverts:** Concrete gutters obstructed by silt, debris, or broken slabs causing road flooding.
- **Malfunctioning Tidal Floodgates / Sluice Gates:** Broken rubber flaps or stuck steel gate controls allowing tidal seawater to backflow into residential streets.
- **Damaged Dikes and River Revetment Walls:** Seepage, structural cracks, or soil scouring along river dikes in Paliwas, Tawiran, Paco, or Binuangan.
- **Defective Public Streetlights:** Unlit or flickering solar/LED streetlights that create pedestrian safety and crime risks at night.
- **Broken Bridge Railings:** Damaged safety barriers on pedestrian or vehicular bridges across the Obando river network.

---

## Step-by-Step Reporting Procedure

### Step 1: Document the Infrastructure Damage

Collect key details to help engineering evaluators dispatch the right materials and crew:

- **Specific Location:** Street name, barangay, house number, nearest landmark, or bridge name.
- **Nature and Severity of Damage:** (e.g., "Deep pothole across southbound lane approximately 1 meter wide" or "Drainage manhole cover missing").
- **Photographs:** Take clear photos showing both the close-up defect and the wider street context.

### Step 2: File the Repair Request

#### Channel A: Direct to Obando Municipal Engineering Office

- Visit the **Municipal Engineering Office**, 2nd Floor, Obando Municipal Hall, Brgy. Paliwas.
- Submit a written Service Request or fill out the Infrastructure Maintenance Slip.
- Office Trunkline: **(02) 8292-2043** (Ask for the Engineering Maintenance Desk).

#### Channel B: Through the Host Barangay Hall

- Report directly to the **Barangay Committee on Public Works and Infrastructure**.
- The Barangay Captain consolidates and officially endorses local road and drainage repair requests to the Municipal Engineer.

---

## Repair Response Protocol

```
[1. Incident Logging & Triage]
Report logged into the MEO Work Order Tracking System within 24 hours.
       ↓
[2. On-Site Technical Inspection]
A municipal civil engineer inspects the site, checks jurisdictional ownership
(Municipal Road vs. DPWH National Road vs. Private Subdivision),
and prepares a Program of Work (POW) and cost estimate.
       ↓
[3. Safety Barricades & Temporary Patching]
Hazardous potholes or open manholes are barricaded with caution tape
or steel plates within 24–48 hours for immediate motorist protection.
       ↓
[4. Crew Dispatch & Permanent Repair]
Asphalt cold-patching or concrete curing scheduled based on weather and dry tide windows.
       ↓
[5. Completion & Citizen Feedback]
Inspector signs off on structural restoration.
```
