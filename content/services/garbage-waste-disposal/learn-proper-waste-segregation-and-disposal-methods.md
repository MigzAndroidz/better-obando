# Guidelines for Proper Waste Segregation and Disposal in Obando

> Household, commercial, and aquaculture waste management rules under Republic Act 9003 and the Obando Solid Waste Management Code.

---

## Overview

In coastal and low-lying communities like the **Municipality of Obando**, improper waste disposal poses a direct threat to public health and safety. Loose plastic waste, uncollected garbage, and discarded refuse choke drainage waterways, clog tidal gates, aggravate seasonal flooding, and harm fishpond ecosystems in the Obando River and Manila Bay estuaries.

Under **Republic Act 9003 (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act)** and the **Obando Municipal Solid Waste Management Ordinance**, all households, commercial establishments, institutions, and fishpond operators are legally mandated to practice segregation at source.

---

### 1. Biodegradable (Nabubulok)

- **Items:** Leftover food, rice, vegetable and fruit peelings, fish entrails and scales from household prep, eggshells, garden leaves, coffee grounds.
- **Disposal:** Keep in covered green receptacles or biodegradable bags. Where yard space permits, residents are encouraged to practice home backyard composting or pit composting. Can also be surrendered to Barangay MRFs with organic composting pits.

### 2. Recyclable (Naireresiklo)

- **Items:** Clean plastic PET beverage bottles, milk jugs, tin and aluminum cans, glass bottles and jars, cardboard boxes, newspapers, old books, scrap iron/copper/aluminum.
- **Preparation:** Rinse containers with used greywater to remove sweet residue and food smell; dry and flatten boxes to conserve space.
- **Disposal:** Drop off at the **Barangay Materials Recovery Facility (MRF)** or sell directly to accredited community junk shops.

### 3. Residual Waste (Di-Nabubulok na Walang Halagang Pang-ekonomiya)

- **Items:** Single-use plastic sachets (shampoo, coffee, seasoning packets), snack wrappers, worn-out rubber or leather footwear, sanitary napkins, used disposable baby and adult diapers, styrofoam food containers, broken ceramics.
- **Disposal:** Place in durable black trash bags, securely tied. Hand over to the municipal compactor truck on scheduled residual waste collection days.

### 4. Special and Household Hazardous Waste (Mapanganib)

- **Items:** Spent dry cell and lead-acid batteries, fluorescent lamp tubes (CFL/tubes containing mercury), empty paint cans, pesticide bottles, motor oil containers, electronic scrap (busted mobile phones, chargers, appliances), expired pharmaceutical drugs.
- **Disposal:** **Never mix with everyday household trash.** Store separately in a sturdy box and turn over to scheduled Barangay Hazardous/E-Waste Collection Drives or coordinate directly with Obando MENRO.

## Prohibited Acts and Violations

The following practices are strictly unlawful in the Municipality of Obando:

- **Open Burning ("Pagsisiga"):** Burning leaves, plastics, paper, tires, or refuse in yards, streets, or dikes is strictly prohibited by RA 9003 and Republic Act 8749 (Philippine Clean Air Act). It releases toxic dioxins, harms neighbors with asthma, and presents a severe fire hazard in dense communities.
- **Dumping in Waterways and Fishponds:** Discarding trash into riverbanks, canals, estuarine dikes, mangroves, or Manila Bay is penalized with heavy fines, community service, and police citation.
- **Bringing Garbage Out Off-Schedule:** Leaving trash bags on sidewalks or dikes outside of designated collection hours. High tides and stray animals will scatter the bags, creating sanitary hazards.

---

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Pursuant to municipal ordinances:

- **First Offense:** Reprimand, mandatory attendance in a 2-hour solid waste management seminar, or fine of ₱500.
- **Second Offense:** Fine of ₱1,000 and/or 1 to 3 days of mandatory community service (drainage canal cleaning or street sweeping).
- **Third and Subsequent Offenses:** Fine of up to ₱2,500 and/or imprisonment of 1 to 30 days, or revocation of Mayor's Business Permit for commercial establishments.
