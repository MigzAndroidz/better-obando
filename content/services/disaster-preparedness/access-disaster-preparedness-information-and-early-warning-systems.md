# Access Disaster Early Warning Systems and Flood Bulletins in Obando

> Real-time coastal tide forecasts, PAGASA weather bulletins, river sensor gauges, and community siren warnings managed by the Obando MDRRMO.

---

## Overview

Accurate early warning information is essential for protecting life and property in the low-lying **Municipality of Obando**. The **Obando Municipal Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (MDRRMO)** operates a continuous, 24/7 **Emergency Operations Center (EOC)** in Brgy. Paliwas that tracks hydro-meteorological hazards affecting Manila Bay and the Bulacan river networks.

By monitoring satellite telemetry, automated flood depth sensors, NAMRIA astronomical tide tables, and PAGASA rainfall radars, the MDRRMO provides real-time community advisories to residents, fishpond operators, commuters, and school administrators.

---

## Early Warning Systems in Place

### 1. NAMRIA Coastal High-Tide Tracking

- Obando's geography causes coastal water from Manila Bay to surge up the Obando, Binuangan, and Meycauayan rivers during peak astronomical tides.
- When the projected astronomical tide exceeds **1.4 meters above Mean Lower Low Water (MLLW)**, low-lying sections of J.P. Rizal St., Brgy. Paliwas, Tawiran, and Panghulo experience street gutter flooding even on sunny days.
- The MDRRMO publishes daily **Tidal Bulletins** indicating the exact peak high-tide hour and expected water level so residents can schedule trips and protect vehicles.

### 2. River Water Level Gauges & Flood Sensors

- Automated ultrasonic water level sensors are installed along key river choke points and bridges:
  - **Obando River Bridge (Paliwas–Poblacion)**
  - **Tawiran River Crossing**
  - **Panghulo / Polo Boundary Bridge**
  - **Binuangan Estuarine Gauge**
- These sensors transmit real-time water elevation data to the MDRRMO Operations Center, giving advance notice of upstream river swelling from Bulacan mountain headwaters.

### 3. Community Early Warning Sirens

- High-decibel emergency siren sirens are mounted on top of the Obando Municipal Hall and selected barangay hall towers.
- **Siren Warning Signals:**
  - **One Continuous 1-Minute Blast:** Weather Alert / Standby for Preemptive Evacuation.
  - **Intermittent Wailing Blasts (3 Minutes):** Forced Evacuation Warning / Imminent Dike Overflow or Flash Tidal Surge. Proceed immediately to designated evacuation centers.
  - **Single Long Blast (All-Clear):** Conditions have stabilized and floodwaters are receding.

## Public Warning and Communication Channels

Stay informed through official verified municipal sources:

1. **Obando MDRRMO Facebook Page:** Real-time posting of hourly weather outlooks, tide charts, suspension of classes, and flood passability reports for light vehicles along national and municipal roads.
2. **Barangay Public Address Systems & Megaphones:** Barangay Tanods and volunteer responders broadcast voice warnings through residential alleys and coastal dikes prior to peak tides or storm landfall.
3. **Emergency Text Alerts (NDRRMO / Bulacan PDRRMC):** Cell-broadcast emergency alerts sent directly to mobile phones in the geographical area during tropical cyclones and storm surge warnings.
4. **Community Two-Way Radio Network:** Continuous VHF emergency frequency monitoring connecting the municipal EOC with all 11 barangays, PNP, BFP, and coastal Bantay Dagat outposts.

## Class and Work Suspension Guidelines

In accordance with Executive Order No. 66, s. 2012 and DepEd Order No. 037, s. 2022:

- **PAGASA Wind Signal No. 1, 2, or 3:** Automatic in-person class suspension for kindergarten, elementary, and secondary levels.
- **Localized Tidal Inundation / Heavy Rainfall (Orange / Red Warning):** The Municipal Mayor declares localized suspension of classes or government work based on MDRRMO high-tide flood assessments and road passability. Official announcements are released by 4:30 AM (for morning sessions) and by 10:30 AM (for afternoon sessions).
